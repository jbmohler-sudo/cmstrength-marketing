MEET-DAY CHECKLIST

Gear, food, and the order of operations from the night before to your third attempt. Packed once, checked twice.

The Gear Meet-Day Timeline Attempt Selection

The singlet is still in your gym bag from last year's qualifier. The chalk is in the garage, on the shelf above the lawnmower, where you left it in June. You will discover both facts tomorrow at 6:40 in a hotel parking lot, one hand on the trunk, and you will spend the next hour paying for a ten-minute job you should have done the night before.

Meet day is not the day you prove your training is real. That work is done. Meet day is the day you cash it in, and the only thing left to lose is logistics. So don't lose it. Here is the complete list, the order of operations, and the rules that keep a good day from becoming a long one.

The Gear

Ten items. Everything below is either required to compete, required to lift well, or required to survive a nine-hour day on your feet. Tap each item as it lands in the bag.

0 OF 10 PACKED
Pack the night before. Check it twice. Sleep. Everything else on this page is details. That rule is the plan.

The Meet-Day Timeline

A powerlifting meet has a shape. Learn it once and you stop being surprised by it.

1
Weigh-In

Same-day or day-before, depending on the federation. If it's same-day, get there early and weigh in as soon as the window opens. Every minute after the scale is a minute of rehydrating and eating.

2
Rules Briefing

The meet director reads the rules, the head referee explains the commands, and your gear gets a fit check. This is where the wrong singlet gets caught, so this is where you confirm what you packed.

3
Warm-Up Area

Your only chance to feel the weights. Work up to your opener with enough reps to know you're ready and enough left to prove it. Start the ramp roughly 45 minutes before your flight gets called.

4
Your Attempts

Lifts run in flights and the calls come fast. When your name is announced you have a minute to get to the platform, so stay close, stay warm, and know exactly what's next.

5
Between Attempts

This is where meets are won and lost. Hydrate. Eat a few bites of something familiar. Keep your layers on, because a cold lifter pulls lighter. Walk, don't sit for an hour. Replay the attempt you just did once, then let it go.

If this is your first meet, the full arc from first session to the platform is in our guide to powerlifting after 40. It covers the timeline, the training, and what actually changes as you get older.

Attempt Selection Is Strategy

Three attempts per lift, and each one has a job. Your opener is a sure thing: a weight you have hit under control in training, usually in the mid-90s of your best recent number. It exists to put points on the board and blood in your ears.

Your second attempt is your best recent number, the heaviest thing you've moved cleanly in this training block. It is your real competition lift. Your third is the PR, the weight you want rather than the weight you can prove.

Strategy means reading the day. If the opener moves like a warm-up, the second becomes the PR and the third becomes a gift. If the opener grinds, the third backs off to the number that still gets you a total. Nobody wins a meet on the platform they planned in the parking lot, but the ones who planned are the ones who walk off it without regrets.
