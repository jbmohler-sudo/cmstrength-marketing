POWERLIFTING CALCULATOR

TOTAL CALCULATOR

Nine attempts. Three lifts. One number that cannot be argued with. Your total is the only score that matters on a powerlifting platform.

The scoreboard doesn't argue

Powerlifting is a rare sport: the scoreboard cannot lie. The bar went up, or it didn't. Three judges, nine attempts, and the only number that survives the day is your total. That's your best successful squat, plus your best successful bench, plus your best successful deadlift, summed.

Everything else in training is a proxy. Estimated 1RMs, rep PRs, RPE charts, a great week in the gym. On meet day the proxies cash out, and your total is what they cash out to.

Every training block either moves one of those three numbers or it doesn't. A program that can't point to which attempt it's building is just a calendar with weights on it.

One detail most lifters get wrong: only your best lift in each movement counts. Misses don't subtract. Your total is not the sum of everything you touched; it is the sum of your three best successful single attempts.

Enter your three attempts for each lift in the unit you train in. Leave an attempt blank if you passed on it or it didn't count. The calculator takes the best of your three attempts per lift and adds them up. Pure addition, the same way the scoreboard does it.

How to spend nine attempts

Your total is decided before the first attempt is loaded. It's decided in the numbers you write on your card.

Opener: a weight you could hit asleep

It has to be a guarantee. A missed opener burns more than one attempt; it shakes the whole day. Plan for roughly 90 percent of your third, and don't get brave on the day.

Second: your best recent training number

The single you've hit clean in the last few weeks. Expect to make it. Be genuinely annoyed if you don't.

Third: the PR you came for

The number that got you up in the dark. This one is allowed to miss. That's what it's for.

Three rules keep the card honest. Never lower a planned attempt because the warm-up room felt heavy; the platform is different. Never raise a third because the crowd is loud; the plan was made on paper, in daylight. And if the first two attempts went exactly to plan, the third is exactly what you wrote. No hero math.

Attempt selection is intensity management, and it starts months out, not on the platform. If you want your training weights picked so the right number is waiting for you at the meet, read RPE for powerlifting, then let the programming do the math.

Read your total in context

A total is only meaningful relative to your weight class and your federation. Same number, a 60 kg lifter versus a 120 kg lifter: different achievements entirely. That's why weight classes exist, and why the DOT Score and Wilks calculators exist, to level the field across bodyweights. Compare on those terms, or don't compare.

The total is also the one number that can't be gamed. Your gym log can flatter you. Your 1RM estimate can drift. A total is three white lights, or it isn't. That's why it's the number worth training toward.

And the raw number still has to be earned under your federation's rules. Depth, the pause, the command to rack: they all change the total. Train the lift your federation judges.
