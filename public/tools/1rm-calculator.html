The method

How the estimate works

A one rep max is the heaviest weight you can lift for one clean rep. Testing it directly is slow, because every near-max attempt needs full recovery, and past 40 it is often not worth the injury risk.

So this page estimates it from a set you already did. The main number uses the Epley formula, the most common estimator in powerlifting programming:

1RM = WEIGHT × (1 + REPS ÷ 30)

For the tightest estimate, log a hard set of 2 to 6 reps taken close to failure. That set is already near a max effort, so the formula has little to guess. Past about 10 reps the number goes loose, because the set is testing endurance more than maximal strength.

Under each result you will also see the Brzycki estimate. More on why below.

Reps in reserve

Why this calculator asks for RPE

Most 1RM calculators assume every set went to failure. Yours probably did not. A set of 5 that had two more reps in it is not a 5-rep max, and plugging it in as one underestimates you.

RPE fixes that. On the reps-in-reserve scale used in powerlifting, RPE 10 means nothing left, RPE 9 means one rep left, RPE 8 means two [1]. The calculator adds the reps you left back before it runs the formula, so 5 reps at RPE 8 is treated like a 7-rep max. Lifters rate this scale reasonably well, and they get better at it with practice [1][2].

Converting a 3RM or 5RM: multiply the weight by (1 + reps ÷ 30). A 3RM of 275 lb is about a 303 lb max. A 5RM of 225 lb is about 263 lb. Going the other way, divide your max by the same number to find a rep target: a 315 lb squatter should expect around 270 lb for 5.

Formulas

Epley vs Brzycki

FormulaEquationBehavior
Epleyw × (1 + r/30)The powerlifting default. Slightly more generous from low-rep sets.
Brzyckiw × 36 / (37 − r)About 4% lower from a 3 to 5 rep set. Meets Epley at 10 reps.

Neither is "right." They bracket the answer. If you are coming back from a layoff or a bad week, program off the lower number. If the input set was a clean, honest top set, Epley is fine.

Most of your training should live in the 70 to 85% band. That is where strength gets built without the recovery bill of constant heavy singles. Why you don't max out all the time covers the reasoning.

Garbage in, garbage out

How to get an honest estimate

  1. Use a top set, not a back-off set. The first hard set after warm-ups, not set five of the day.
  2. Hold meet standards. Depth on the squat, a pause on the bench, a full lockout on the pull. A high squat inflates the number and the program built on it.
  3. Rate RPE right after the set, before the story you tell yourself gets better.
  4. Recalculate every 3 to 4 weeks, or whenever a top set moves clearly faster than it should.
  5. Save the true max for the platform. Peaking is its own skill. The meet prep guide walks through it.

Lifting after 40? Estimating instead of testing matters even more, because recovery is the constraint, not strength. How to powerlift after 40 lays out the whole approach.

FAQ

Questions lifters ask

01How accurate is a 1RM calculator?

Within a few percent, if the input set was genuinely hard: 2 to 6 reps taken close to failure. Easy sets understate your max by exactly as much as they were easy, which is why this calculator lets you enter RPE.

02What rep range gives the best 1RM estimate?

Two to six reps taken close to failure. That set is close enough to a true max effort that the formula has little to extrapolate. Past about 10 reps the estimate drifts, because you are testing endurance more than maximal strength.

03How do I convert a 3RM or 5RM to a 1RM?

Multiply the weight by (1 + reps / 30). A 3RM of 275 lb works out to about 303 lb. A 5RM of 225 lb works out to about 263 lb. Or enter the set above and the calculator does it for you.

04How does RPE change the 1RM estimate?

RPE tells the calculator how many reps you left in the tank. RPE 10 means none, RPE 9 means one, RPE 8 means two. The calculator adds those reps back before running the formula, so 5 reps at RPE 8 is treated like a 7-rep max.

05What percentage of my 1RM is 5 reps?

About 86% by the Epley formula, if the set is taken to failure. At RPE 8, 5 reps lands closer to 81%.

06Should I test my true 1RM instead of estimating it?

For programming, no. An estimate drives training percentages just fine, and you skip the fatigue and injury risk of maxing out. Save true maxes for the platform.

07Which formula does this 1RM calculator use?

Epley, 1RM = weight x (1 + reps / 30), as the main number, with Brzycki, 1RM = weight x 36 / (37 - reps), shown alongside. Brzycki reads a few percent lower from low-rep sets, and the two meet at 10 reps.
