The method

How the estimate works

A one rep max is the heaviest weight you can lift for one clean rep. Testing it directly is slow — every near-max attempt needs full recovery between tries — and past 40 it is often not worth the injury risk.

So this page estimates it instead. The Epley formula is the most widely used estimator in powerlifting programming:

1RM = WEIGHT × (1 + REPS ÷ 30)

For the tightest estimate, log a challenging set of 2–6 reps taken close to failure. Past about 10 reps the number goes loose — at that point the set is testing endurance more than maximal strength.

FAQ

Questions lifters ask

01How accurate is a 1RM calculator?

Within a few percent, if the input set was genuinely challenging — 2 to 6 reps taken close to failure. Easy sets understate your max by exactly as much as they were easy.

02What rep range gives the best 1RM estimate?

Two to six reps taken close to failure. The formulas agree closely in that range. Past about 10 reps the estimate drifts, because you're testing endurance more than maximal strength.

03Should I test my true 1RM instead of estimating it?

For programming purposes, no need. An estimate drives training percentages just fine, and you skip the fatigue and injury risk of maxing out. Save true maxes for the platform.

04Which formula does this 1RM calculator use?

The Epley formula: 1RM = weight × (1 + reps ÷ 30). It is the most widely used estimator in powerlifting programming.
